# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

People around Guildford and Western Sydney who love Pakistani desserts, mostly on phones: checking this week's dish from an Instagram link, standing on a footpath looking for the truck, or joining a list for a dessert that is not on yet. A smaller group arrives on laptops from search, catering enquiries and events.

Fizz is the second user. She runs the week from her phone next to a hot stove, so the admin (a separate product surface at `/admin`) must let her change what is live, see orders and message her list in under two minutes.

## Product Purpose

Sweet Munchies by Fizz is a family-run Pakistani dessert truck that drops one or two hero dishes a week. The site treats each weekly drop as an event: every dessert stays visible, the live ones sell through Square, and the rest capture a Notify me sign-up that brings people back when they return. Success is people opening `/this-week`, wanting the dish, and either ordering or joining the list, plus Fizz running it without help.

## Brand Personality

Cheeky, warm, generous. A Pakistani dessert truck with the swagger of a luxury confectionery launch: something is always being unwrapped. The voice sits between an aunty insisting on a second helping and a friend texting that the good stuff is back. Short lines, real food words (say kheer, not rice pudding). The logo's glossy rose and chocolate swirl leads; Pakistani truck art is seasoning in borders, stamps and small moments, never wallpaper.

## Anti-references

- A plain menu with nice photos, the default dessert-shop website.
- Corporate, "artisanal" or precious; minimal for the sake of it.
- Explaining Pakistani desserts like a museum label.
- Truck art used as a costume or behind body text.
- Stock dessert photography or anything that looks generic.
- Anything copied from FIX Dessert Chocolatier: its models, wrappers, photos, copy, product names or fonts.
- Look-alike Biscoff or Bueno packaging.

## Design Principles

1. The drop is an event: the weekly dish is unwrapped, not listed.
2. Unavailable is still desirable: sealed desserts stay beautiful and always offer Notify me, never greyed out.
3. Fun in the display, facts plain: names, places, times and allergens are always clear to a stranger, a screen reader and a search engine.
4. Phone first and always interruptible: every moment works on a phone in the sun, and nobody is ever stuck watching an animation.
5. Real content under every effect: the 3D, loader and intro are layers over real HTML.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text in cocoa on paper; rose only for large display text. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (no intro, no parallax, static shelf). Full keyboard use with visible focus. Tap targets at least 44px. Everything works without WebGL or JavaScript. Allergens are always real text. Sound is off unless the user deliberately turns it on. Australian English throughout, no em or en dashes in copy.
